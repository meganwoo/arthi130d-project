---
title: "More details about their geography and context"
---

Geographically, the vessels are found in desert coastal regions. In these communities, the ability to retain and store fluids is presumably a valuable asset. The unique shape of these vessels is thought to function, in part, as a method of mitigating the amount of liquid lost to evaporation. This is corroborated by the desert surroundings of these artifacts. Stirrup-spout artifacts are prevalent in archaeological sites along a 250-mi stretch of the northern Peruvian coastline.

Stirrup-spout vessels are most concentrated within burial sites of influential community figures but fragments are occasionally found in homes. Preservation of such intricate structures in a non-burial context is much rarer, and the nature of the manufacturing process likely made these valuable commodities that were only available to upper class families.
